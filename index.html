A crowded, temperate system built for atmosphere-hunting

TRAPPIST-1 is a system of seven roughly Earth-sized rocky planets around an ultracool M dwarf, discovered by the TRAPPIST, Spitzer, and K2 surveys and refined through years of follow-up. Planet e sits in the conventional habitable zone, and its small, cool, nearby host star gives an unusually favorable transit depth for atmospheric spectroscopy on a rocky planet — one of the reasons it's a flagship JWST target despite its Earth-like size.

Espinoza et al. (2025), part of the JWST-TST DREAMS program, combined four NIRSpec/PRISM transits of TRAPPIST-1e and corrected the resulting spectrum for contamination from unocculted starspots and faculae on the host star — a real, significant systematic for any planet transiting an active M dwarf, since spots and faculae imprint their own wavelength-dependent signal on the transit depth independent of the planet's atmosphere.

Final spectrum and reduction-sensitivity audit

The final product does not reject a fitted constant under this diagonal-error likelihood. Its reduced χ² of 0.649 and standardized-residual RMS of 0.800 may reflect conservative marginal errors and/or covariance that this calculation omits; p = 0.988 is not proof that no atmosphere exists. Across all six reductions, visit 104 is lower than visits 001–003; its median weighted depth is 4715.2 ppm, 360.2 ppm below the median of the earlier visit medians, and its between-pipeline range reaches 160.0 ppm. This is a visit-level systematic-sensitivity diagnostic, not a planetary-atmosphere detection. Espinoza et al. (2025)'s full retrieval—not this audit—rules out cloudy primary atmospheres with at least 80% H2 by volume at better than 3σ.

Data and method notes

System parameters come from the NASA Exoplanet Archive TAP service. The final product and the 24 pipeline/visit tables come from the authors' Zenodo archive—see data/SOURCE.md and the pipeline-product provenance. The complete audit is in scripts/analyze_spectrum.py; machine-readable results are available as JSON and CSV. These descriptive diagonal-error fits neither reproduce the paper's covariance model nor perform atmospheric retrieval or model selection.
